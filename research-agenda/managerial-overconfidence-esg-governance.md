# Research Agenda

## Managerial Overconfidence and Strategic Green Risk Allocation in Emerging Asia: The Guardrail Role of ESG Governance Architecture

### Status: Proposed research direction

## Background and Motivation

Managerial characteristics may influence how organizations evaluate uncertainty and allocate resources. In emerging Asian markets, corporate governance and environmental, social, and governance (ESG) arrangements provide a relevant context for examining how executive decision-making relates to strategic risk allocation.

This research direction builds on my academic interests in behavioral finance, managerial overconfidence, corporate governance, and strategic risk-taking.

## Core Research Question

How might ESG governance architecture shape the relationship between managerial overconfidence and strategic green risk allocation in emerging Asian markets?

## Potential Research Themes

- Managerial overconfidence and executive decision-making
- Corporate governance mechanisms
- ESG governance architecture
- Strategic green risk allocation
- Emerging Asian markets

## Potential Contribution

The proposed research aims to connect behavioral finance with corporate governance and strategic sustainability decisions. Its theoretical contribution, empirical design, variables, and expected findings remain to be developed and validated through further literature review and research design.

## Research Status

This page documents a proposed research direction. It is not a completed study, published paper, or claim of established empirical findings.
